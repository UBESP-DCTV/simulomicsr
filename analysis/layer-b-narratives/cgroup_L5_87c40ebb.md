## Biological context

Interferon gamma (IFN-γ), the only type II interferon, comes mainly from activated T and NK cells, signalling through IFNGR1/IFNGR2 and JAK1/JAK2 to phosphorylate STAT1; STAT1 homodimers bind gamma-activated sequences, and IRF1, an immediate STAT1 target, extends it to interferon-stimulated response elements [1,2]. Genome-wide surveys separated type II from type I targets [3].

The characteristic programme comprises antigen processing and presentation (TAP1 and the immunoproteasome for MHC class I [4], MHC class II via CIITA [5]), cell-autonomous defence effectors led by the guanylate-binding proteins [6], the CXCR3 ligands CXCL9/10/11 [7], and counter-regulatory genes such as IDO1 [8]. The same signature underlies mRNA profiles predicting response to PD-1 blockade [9]; its amplitude depends on cell type and prior state [1].

## What the meta-analysis shows

The comparison pools 20 studies, with 3579 significant genes (FDR < 0.05) among 18948 tested. Most contrast IFN-γ with untreated or vehicle-treated controls; three do not (below). All five targets fixed from the literature in advance are recovered with the expected positive sign: STAT1 (log2 fold change +2.894, FDR 2.59e-91, 20 studies), GBP1 (+5.977, 1.35e-58, 20 studies), CXCL9 (+11.271, 1.04e-51, 15 studies), IRF1 (+4.449, 1.15e-35, 20 studies) and TAP1 (+4.212, 1.01e-18, 20 studies).

Two families are recovered on high study counts: the CXCR3 chemokines (CXCL11 +9.364, CXCL10 +8.986; 15 and 16 studies) and the guanylate-binding proteins (GBP5 +8.583, GBP4 +5.954, both in all 20), with IDO1 (+9.795, FDR 2.05e-33, 17 studies). MHC class II is induced on fewer studies, and values depend on which of the several Ensembl identifiers sharing a symbol here is read: HLA-DRA gives +12.228 in 2 studies at the canonical identifier and +5.566 in 11 at others, HLA-DPB1 +3.034 (FDR 1.04e-05) in 9.

## Interpretation and limits

Under the random-effects weights used for pooling, no single contributor carries the result: the effective number of studies is 18.16 of 20 (91%) and the heaviest, GSE144484, holds 6.59% of the weight — 16.9% under within-study precision alone, still far from half. A reading of the comparisons found one contrast, with three exceptions among the 35: two carry the MMP inhibitor SB-3CT, one a *Chlamydia trachomatis* co-infection that induces part of the same programme on its own; those two studies hold 13.0% of the weight. Five comparisons use perturbed backgrounds (IRF1 knockout, PTPN2 targeting, a MED12 knock-in, BET-inhibitor-treated K562), the perturbation on both arms so the pairing holds; one contribution to the IRF1 estimate is thus from IRF1-deficient cells. Heterogeneity is the main limitation: the median I² is 90.51, highest for genes measured in all 20 studies (IRF1 99.7, STAT1 99.5, GBP1 99.5, TAP1 99.8), though the pooled estimates remain positive and highly significant. This is consistent with, though not tested here, the diversity of the systems pooled: epithelial, haematopoietic and endothelial lines, colonic organoids, mesenchymal stromal cells and trophoblast stem cells, at differing doses and times. Some of the most significant entries rest on few studies: GIMAP4 (+2.063) and GIMAP7 (+3.044), each from 2 studies with I² 0.0, rank above genes measured in all 20. Fifteen further studies matched the contrast but had no usable within-study comparison, a technical criterion that can concentrate rather than dilute error. No study contributed patient tissue (2 in vitro models, 18 unclassified), so the results describe a cell-intrinsic response, not IFN-γ signalling in vivo.
