## Biological context

JQ1 occupies the acetyl-lysine binding pocket of the BET proteins BRD2, BRD3, BRD4 and BRDT and displaces them from acetylated chromatin [1]. Because BRD4 delivers P-TEFb to promoters and enhancers, its displacement acts on transcriptional elongation, and the changes are selective rather than global: the best characterised is loss of MYC transcription [2], attributed to the dependence of a few genes on large enhancer domains densely occupied by BRD4 and Mediator [3]. A mechanistically distinct response is the induction of HEXIM1: JQ1 transiently releases free P-TEFb from the inhibitory 7SK small nuclear ribonucleoprotein, of which HEXIM1 is a subunit, and HEXIM1 transcription rises in consequence [4]; it is the most reproducible pharmacodynamic marker of BET target engagement [5]. In activated macrophages, BET inhibition preferentially silences secondary-response inflammatory genes while housekeeping transcription is spared [6]. The programmes it dismantles can be re-established through alternative regulators, so responding genes vary by lineage [7].

## What the meta-analysis shows

Twenty-five of the 41 studies recording the treatment contributed to the pooled comparison of JQ1 against vehicle or untreated controls; 9678 of 19105 genes tested reached FDR < 0.05. No target genes were specified in advance; the genes below are reported as observed. Effects measured in all 25 studies are small, and each shares the sign of the summary in 24 or 25 of them: TMEM242 +0.637 (FDR 1.04e-66), ZNF445 +0.752 (9.75e-48), KCTD21 +0.984 (2.14e-39) and CALM1 +0.780 (3.05e-37) increase, FUNDC1 -0.812 (3.56e-35) and C3orf33 -1.489 (8.13e-32) decrease. HEXIM1 is induced, +1.732 (9.10e-29), in all 25 studies with the sign shared by 24, the expected pharmacodynamic response [5]; MYC is not significant (+0.225, FDR 0.44, 24 studies, sign shared by 14). Twenty-nine of the thirty largest effects are decreases, led by OR51E1 -6.463 (1.8e-08, k=2) and SLAMF8 -4.827 (2.86e-07, k=4); the one large increase, H3C14 +4.032 (1.74e-10), is the only one of the thirty measured in 20 studies. Chemokine and immune-recognition genes fall: CCR1 -3.991 (1.23e-11, k=8), CD244 -2.071 (3.70e-53, k=3) and CCR2 -4.002 (0.0244, k=2).

## Interpretation and limits

Weight is spread: 23.70 effective studies of 25 (95% of the nominal count), the largest single contribution 4.81% (GSE113604), so no single experiment determines the summary. The median I-squared of 95.59% indicates large differences in effect size; agreement on direction is a separate property, measured only for the genes named above. The material is mixed — 3 in vitro models, 1 patient tissue, 21 not classified — and spans several lineages, doses and exposure times. Six of the 58 pooled comparisons, carrying 3.2% of the weight, pair a treated culture from the pons with a control from another brain region. The response is broad but not globally repressive: 5440 of the 9678 significant genes decrease and 4238 increase, and among those measured in all 25 studies the majority increase (3088 of 5338). The largest fold changes rest on as few as two studies and several have FDR close to the threshold. The fall in CCR1, CCR2, SLAMF8 and CD244 runs in the direction reported for BET inhibition of inflammatory transcription [6], but that work concerns activated macrophages, a context absent here: the correspondence is an analogy, not a replication. The comparison shows a broad response to JQ1 with a small, directionally consistent shared component, and does not establish lineage-specific effect sizes.
