## Biological context

5alpha-dihydrotestosterone (DHT) is the highest-affinity endogenous ligand of the androgen receptor (AR), formed from testosterone by 5alpha-reductase. Ligand binding drives AR nuclear translocation, binding to androgen response elements and coregulator recruitment [1]; in androgen-responsive prostate adenocarcinoma cells only a restricted subset of the transcriptome responds [2]. That subset includes canonical, directly bound targets: KLK3, whose induction in LNCaP cells is mediated principally by DHT rather than testosterone [3]; TMPRSS2, prostate-restricted and androgen-regulated [4]; FKBP5, controlled by an AR-bound enhancer 65 kb downstream of its transcription start site [5]; and NKX3-1, itself androgen-induced [6]. AR is also active outside the prostate, in AR-positive, ER-negative breast carcinoma lines [7] and in dermal papilla cells engineered to express AR [8]. The AR cistrome is itself reprogrammed between normal and tumour prostate under pioneer factor control [9], so a pooled response is expected to mix a canonical core with context-dependent components.

## What the meta-analysis shows

The comparison pools 23 studies setting DHT exposure against a vehicle or untreated arm; eleven further studies with DHT comparisons were not pooled, having no control measured within the same study. 4875 of 19186 tested genes reach FDR < 0.05, at a median I-squared of 85.05%. All four canonical AR targets specified before the analysis are recovered with the expected sign: FKBP5 log2 fold change +2.322 (FDR 5.35e-08, 23 studies), KLK3 +2.267 (7.21e-06, 14), TMPRSS2 +1.780 (6.49e-08, 19) and NKX3-1 +1.368 (1.28e-05, 23), each with I-squared 99.7 to 99.9. The head of the significance ranking is mixed: its three leading entries are large in amplitude but rest on few studies — AFP -2.000 (2.57e-44, 5 studies, I-squared 0.0), COL22A1 +4.412 (6.65e-28, 2 studies, 0.0) and KRT72 +5.405 (6.65e-28, 6 studies, 77.4) — while immediately below them are small shifts measured in all 23 studies, UCHL3 +0.335 (3.39e-23, I-squared 44.7) and SLC25A33 +0.602 (3.54e-18). Large-amplitude changes are distributed across the ranking rather than confined to its foot: PGC +4.630 (4.54e-20, 17 studies), whose promoter carries an element responsive to androgens, glucocorticoids and progesterone in breast carcinoma cells [10]; and SLC38A4 +4.147 (1.13e-08, 19 studies).

## Interpretation and limits

The pooled estimate reproduces the direction of the canonical agonist program under four constraints. Single-study leverage depends on the accounting used: 20.91 of 23 studies are effective under Kish weighting, where the heaviest study GSE125014 carries 6.10% of the weight, whereas under a per-study median inverse-variance weighting it reaches 22.48%, and the two heaviest together 36.7%. The material is not clinical: 12 studies are in vitro models, none is patient tissue, and for the remaining 11 the material is not determined, so the estimate cannot be read as describing prostate tissue in vivo. Heterogeneity is high (median I-squared 85.05%, 99.7 to 99.9 on the canonical targets), so pooled magnitudes are not transportable between studies; direction, measured separately, is shared by most contributors — across the four canonical targets 71 of 79 per-study estimates carry the sign of the pooled estimate (89.9%). Finally, although every pooled comparison sets DHT against an untreated or vehicle arm, 3 of the 44 comparisons, carrying 12.0% of the weight, do not isolate DHT alone: DHT is given together with the AR antagonist enzalutamide in 22RV1 cells (GSE130247), with estradiol in T47D cells (GSE99626), and with a change of culture substrate in MDaPCa2a cells (GSE227511). The pooled studies also differ in cell line, receptor genotype and exposure duration (1 h to 96 h), and several of the largest effects rest on two studies each (COL22A1, TRIM10).
