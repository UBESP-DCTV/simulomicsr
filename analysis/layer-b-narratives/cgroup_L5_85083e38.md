## Biological context

Parkinson disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder defined by loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra and by intraneuronal α-synuclein aggregation [1]. Its genetics span rare recessive forms, including loss of *PRKN* (parkin), and many common risk loci [2]. Parkin acts with PINK1 to mark damaged mitochondria for autophagic clearance, recruiting receptors that engage LC3 and nucleate autophagosomes [5].

Post-mortem transcriptomics leads one to expect predominant downregulation in affected regions, in neuron-enriched rather than glial transcripts [3], with suppression of ATP-metabolic programmes across Braak stages and altered axonal maintenance at advanced stages [4]. Stem-cell models carry a different expectation: hiPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons from two carriers of identical *PRKN* deletions but discordant clinical presentation differ substantially in transcriptome and metabolome, so such a contrast carries donor and line background alongside the mutation [6].

## What the meta-analysis shows

Eleven case-versus-control studies were assessed and ten retained: 976 of 19,025 tested genes are significant at FDR < 0.05, with a median I² of 64.20%. No target genes were fixed in advance, so results are reported descriptively.

All thirty of the most significant estimates have an I² of 0.0 (ACOT7, 0.8) against that median: at two or three studies the between-study variance is estimated as zero, the standard error collapses and the FDR follows, so this ranking favours thin coverage. Several leading genes rest on two or three studies — TMSB15A (log2 fold change −0.730, FDR 3.94e-158, 2 studies), NEUROG2 (−1.075, 5.27e-96, 3), HAND2 (+3.854, 4.65e-89, 2) — as does the largest effect (RBM46 +5.828, 9.16e-13, 2). Twelve of the thirty rest on seven to nine studies and do not move together: three synaptic or axon-guidance transcripts are reduced (NRXN3 −0.385, 6.96e-38; RIMBP2 −0.529, 1.3e-36; ROBO2 −0.451, 2.74e-35), while BRD2 (+0.661, 3.48e-40) and CHRNA3 (+0.568, 1.24e-26) are increased and others belong to unrelated programmes (FADS1 −0.183, 4.4e-26). Cell-cycle transcripts are reduced throughout (CDC20 −0.546, 6.55e-135, 7 studies; BIRC5 −0.386, 1.5e-59, 5; CEP55 −0.415, 1.98e-54, 5). OPTN, an autophagy receptor recruited downstream of parkin [5], is the only gene among the thirty measured in all ten studies (−0.218, FDR 4.48e-32, I² 0.0); noted after the fact, it supports no mechanistic claim.

## Interpretation and limits

The pooled comparisons all contrast Parkinson cases against unaffected controls, but their pairing was not audited: one 88-year-old case is set against controls aged 66 and 72. The pooled result is close to a single-study estimate — 1.79 effective studies of the ten pooled (18%) — with GSE181029, an induced pluripotent stem cell model of *PARK2* exon deletions differentiated to neural progenitors and dopaminergic-like neurons, carrying 73.16% of the weight (55.03% under a per-gene inverse-variance weighting; dominant either way). The material is mixed in kind (2 in vitro models, 1 patient tissue, 7 unclassified) and in anatomy: one study contributes amygdala, medial temporal gyrus and substantia nigra separately, two contribute stem-cell-derived neurons, most declare no tissue. The developmental transcription factors among the leading genes (NEUROG2, HAND2, OLIG3 −1.775, FDR 7.24e-29, 2 studies) and the reduced cell-cycle transcripts fit differentiation-state differences between mutant and control lines better than post-mortem nigral biology; the per-study composition of single-gene estimates is not resolved here, so that attribution is inferential. Only the broadly covered estimates admit a cross-study reading, and these too lean on the dominant in vitro model.
