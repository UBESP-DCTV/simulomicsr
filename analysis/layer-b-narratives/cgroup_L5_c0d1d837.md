## Biological context

Enzalutamide is a second-generation androgen receptor (AR) antagonist: it binds the AR ligand-binding domain with high affinity and blocks nuclear translocation, coactivator recruitment and chromatin binding, without the residual agonist activity of first-generation antiandrogens [1]; it prolongs overall survival in metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer [2]. AR is a ligand-activated transcription factor driving a defined transcriptional programme in prostate cells [3], so blockade is expected to cause a coordinate loss of AR-dependent transcripts, of which KLK3, TMPRSS2, FKBP5 and NKX3-1 are the usual readouts; suppression of TMPRSS2 by enzalutamide has also been shown outside the prostate [4]. Two qualifications follow: enzalutamide-induced transcripts are largely not the mirror image of androgen-induced ones [5], and AR blockade engages bypass programmes — glucocorticoid receptor substitution [6] and, in TP53/RB1-deficient backgrounds, lineage plasticity [7] — so suppression depth varies between models.

## What the meta-analysis shows

The comparison pools 19 studies contrasting enzalutamide-treated samples with vehicle or untreated controls; of 29 studies identified as treating with enzalutamide, 10 did not meet the pooling requirements. 3498 genes reach FDR < 0.05. All four pre-specified AR target genes are recovered with a negative sign: NKX3-1 (log2 fold change -1.239, FDR 2.57e-04, 19 studies), KLK3 (-1.600, FDR 3.06e-04, 15 studies), FKBP5 (-1.223, FDR 3.15e-03, 19 studies) and TMPRSS2 (-0.922, FDR 7.51e-03, 18 studies). Other large negative effects measured in most pooled studies are SLC38A4 (-4.232, FDR 7.51e-04, 12 studies), PGC (-3.039, FDR 1.52e-04, 13 studies), CHRNA2 (-2.581, FDR 4.39e-03, 16 studies) and ST6GALNAC1 (-2.455, FDR 6.37e-03, 16 studies). The genes with the smallest FDR are of a different kind: COL22A1 (+6.397, FDR 1.71e-61), OR5B12 (-4.709, FDR 1.35e-50), ORM2 (-5.891, FDR 4.38e-41) and TMEM132C (+5.082, FDR 1.63e-24) are each measured in two or three studies, whereas transcripts measured in nearly all studies reach high significance with small effects: USP27X (+0.236, FDR 5.22e-15, 18 studies), SEC22C (-0.238, FDR 2.76e-12, 19 studies).

## Interpretation and limits

The direction of the four canonical readouts is what competitive AR blockade predicts, and it does not rest on one contributor: in the pooled weighting the effective number of studies is 17.37 of 19 (91% of the nominal count) and the largest single-study share of the weight is 7.0%. Heterogeneity is high: the median I-squared is 87.88% and the four canonical targets carry I-squared between 99.8% and 99.9%, so between-study differences in effect magnitude far exceed sampling error. Agreement in direction, which heterogeneity of magnitude does not settle either way, was measured on the per-study estimates: across the four canonical targets 58 of 71 carry the sign of the pooled estimate (81.7%), the lowest being TMPRSS2 at 13 of 18. The material is entirely experimental — 4 studies classified as in vitro models, 15 unclassified, none patient tissue — and comprises prostate cancer lines, xenografts and patient-derived xenografts plus two breast lines (HCC38, MDA-MB-453); several contrasts use engineered backgrounds expected to be less AR-dependent, including LNCaP RB1/TP53 double knockouts and a TLE3 knockout. Four of the 45 contributing comparisons, all from one study carrying about 12% of the weight, pair treated cells of one line against vehicle-treated cells of another (LAPC4 or LNCaP versus VCaP), so there the drug effect is confounded with the difference between lines. Both rankings above are biased: with two or three studies the between-study variance is not estimable and collapses towards zero (I-squared 0.0 for COL22A1), contracting the standard error and making the FDR optimistically small, while the largest effects include transcripts with no documented androgen dependence and I-squared above 95 (TRIM49D2, OR5B2, GHRHR).
