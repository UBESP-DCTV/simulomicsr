## Biological context

SARS-CoV-2 enters cells through ACE2 and TMPRSS2 and has been detected in the respiratory tract, intestine and vasculature, including vessels within the heart and brain [4]. Cytosolic RNA sensing canonically induces type I and type III interferons, IRF-mediated amplification and interferon-stimulated gene expression. In differentiated airway epithelium the type III interferons are induced ahead of IFNB1, and interferon-stimulated genes such as MX1 and ISG15 are expressed in uninfected bystander cells [2]; OAS1 is itself a restriction factor whose antiviral activity requires a prenylated isoform [3]. Profiling against other respiratory viruses described the interferon arm as muted while chemokines were disproportionately induced [1]; circulating CXCL10 at admission predicts clinical outcome [5]. A two-part signature is therefore expected, with amplitude depending on cell type, viral dose and time.

## What the meta-analysis shows

The comparison pools 34 studies of SARS-CoV-2 infection against mock-infected or untreated controls (38 assessed, 34 retained); 1933 of 20723 tested genes reach FDR < 0.05. All four interferon-stimulated genes specified as expected targets before the analysis are recovered with the expected sign: IFIT1 log2 fold change +2.198 (FDR 5.12e-05, 33 studies), ISG15 +1.641 (4.33e-05, 34), MX1 +1.892 (2.86e-04, 34) and OAS1 +1.308 (6.13e-03, 34). Other components of the axis appear at comparable breadth: the regulators IRF7 +1.326 (3.05e-07, 32) and IRF9 +0.737 (2.04e-06, 34), and the effectors TRIM69 +0.729 (2.07e-06, 34) and RSAD2 +2.882 (1.29e-04, 28). The interferons carry the largest pooled effects but rest on fewer studies: IFNB1 +6.964 (5.54e-15, 8), IFNL2 +5.339 (1.25e-06, 7), IFNL1 +4.957 (4.68e-04, 9) and IFNL3 +4.864 (3.91e-04, 7). The chemokine arm is also present: CXCL11 +3.560 (1.76e-05, 23), CXCL10 +3.469 (6.03e-04, 24), CXCL3 +1.509 (2.98e-06, 33) and CSF3 +2.962 (1.81e-03, 21).

## Interpretation and limits

Both arms are recovered across studies not designed together, under several constraints. Between-study heterogeneity is extreme (median I-squared 95.63%, the canonical targets 99.3-99.7): these are mean effects across a heterogeneous population, not amplitudes transferable to a given tissue; directional concordance, measured on the per-study estimates, is nonetheless high: across the four canonical targets 114 of 135 carry the sign of the pooled estimate (84.4%), from 79% for MX1 to 94% for IFIT1. Unmodelled contributors include multiplicity of infection, time, cell type and the viruses used — among them ORF8- and ARKSAP-deletion mutants and a passage-adapted isolate. Neither ranking is neutral: ordering by FDR rewards low heterogeneity, placing genes measured in few studies first (KEL, FDR 8.44e-46, 7 studies, I-squared 0.0; C17orf98, 7.64e-25, 2 studies), and ordering by effect size favours the same genes: 25 of the thirty largest come from genes measured in eleven or fewer of the 34 studies, the interferons among them. Under random-effects weighting the pooled weight is not concentrated (heaviest study 3.80%, 30.25 effective studies of 34), but that follows from the weighting at this heterogeneity, not from comparable information across studies: by within-study precision alone the heaviest accounts for 14.33%. The material is mixed — 11 studies are in vitro models, 2 patient tissue, 21 unclassified — and 3 of 98 comparisons (3.1%, 0.7% of the weight; GSE151803, GSE169241) set patient lung or heart material against a cell-model control rather than a matched mock. Infection is contrasted only with mock, with no second virus, so the design cannot address the relative balance of the two arms [1]. The results are best read as evidence that both programs are induced on average, not as calibrated amplitudes.
