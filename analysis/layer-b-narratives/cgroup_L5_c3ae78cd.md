## Biological context

Crohn's disease is a chronic, relapsing, transmural inflammation of the gut driven by a defective mucosal response to the intestinal microbiota in genetically predisposed hosts [1]. Profiling of affected mucosa has converged on a reproducible programme of epithelial antimicrobial induction, myeloid and neutrophil expansion, acute-phase signalling and matrix remodelling. In treatment-naive paediatric ileal disease from the RISK inception cohort, DUOX2 induction and APOA1 suppression were the most prominent components of a core signature [2]. IL-22 is elevated in inflamed Crohn's lesions and induces IL-8, TNF-alpha and beta-defensin-2 in intestinal epithelium [3], and induces DUOX2 in human intestinal organoids [4]. Collagenase and stromelysin transcripts rise in inflamed colon and track the histological degree of inflammation [5]. CHI3L1 is induced in colonic epithelium and lamina propria during colitis, promoting bacterial adhesion and invasion [6]; NPSR1, an inflammatory bowel disease susceptibility locus, is upregulated in inflamed intestine [7].

## What the meta-analysis shows

The comparison pools 10 studies and reports 3515 of 19184 tested genes as significant at FDR < 0.05, with a median I-squared of 62.91%. No target genes were fixed in advance, so the genes below are described, not treated as a prespecified check. The most significant estimates are NETO2 (+0.692, FDR 1.86e-25, 10 studies), APOL4 (+1.066, 8.2e-25, 10 studies) and UGT1A3 (-3.187, 1.09e-21, 6 studies). Components of the expected programme appear: DUOXA2 (+4.433, 1.62e-14, 8 studies), IL22 (+4.529, 4.26e-17, 2 studies), NPSR1 (+4.358, 1.7e-19, 3 studies), NLRC4 (+0.730, 3.56e-14, 10 studies) and FCGR1A (+2.436, 3.72e-13, 10 studies); DUOX2 itself and APOA1 are not among the genes reported here, so neither half of that expectation can be checked directly. Among the largest effects a neutrophil-recruitment axis appears — CXCL5 (+4.674, 3.96e-4), CXCR1 (+3.350, 4.46e-3), AQP9 (+3.894, 1.77e-4) — with the acute-phase transcript SAA2 (+3.217, 2.82e-4), the constitutive isoform SAA4 (+3.811, 4.26e-17, 3 studies), matrix remodelling (MMP3 +3.788, 4.86e-3; MMP10 +3.491, 1.06e-4) and CHI3L1 (+3.326, 2.47e-3).

## Interpretation and limits

Direction and identity are consistent with the published mucosal signature of active disease, with antimicrobial, myeloid and matrix-degrading genes all represented. That consistency is not fully independent of [2]: the heaviest contributing study draws on the same RISK ileal cohort from which the signature was derived [8], so subject overlap is likely and part of the agreement is circular. Of 16 studies identified, 10 entered the estimate and behave under inverse-variance weighting as 6.87 effective studies, 69% of the nominal count; GSE93624 carries the largest single share, 20.30% under random-effects weighting and roughly twice that if between-study variance is disregarded. Every comparison contrasts patient tissue against healthy or non-IBD control tissue, with no in vitro model (5 patient-tissue, 5 unclassified), but they span ileal mucosa, colonic mucosa and wall, and mesenteric adipose tissue, so part of the between-study variance is likely anatomical rather than pathological. Two comparisons are mismatched internally — colonic segments against sigmoid-colon controls in one study, a sorted CD4 population against whole tissue in another — both from small contributors; no systematic audit of such mismatches was made. Heterogeneity is substantial, and the largest effects carry the highest I-squared (CXCR1 94.7, CHI3L1 94.0, AQP9 93.3, CXCL5 93.2), so those magnitudes are not transferable across settings even where the sign is consistent. Some prominent genes rest on few studies (IL22 on 2; NPSR1 and SAA4 on 3).
