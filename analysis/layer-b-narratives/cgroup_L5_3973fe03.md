## Biological context

IL-1α and IL-1β are the two agonists of the type I interleukin-1 receptor and are approximately equipotent at it [1]. They differ in biogenesis: IL-1α is a constitutive nuclear precursor in epithelial and mesenchymal cells, released on necrotic death as an alarmin, whereas IL-1β requires inflammasome processing [2]. Engagement of IL-1R1 recruits MyD88 and activates NF-κB with the JNK and p38 cascades, inducing IL6, CXCL8, PTGS2 and the feedback inhibitor NFKBIA [3]. In epithelia this core response extends to an antimicrobial, psoriasis-associated module — CCL20, IL36G and DEFB4 — whose induction is abolished by MyD88 deletion [4]; in androgen-receptor-positive prostate carcinoma cells IL-1 also induces inflammatory and interferon-related programmes [7]. Because both ligands act through the same receptor, their transcriptional outputs are expected to overlap extensively, though not compared directly here. Co-stimulation with IFN-γ adds a STAT1/IRF-driven interferon-stimulated module and synergises with IL-1β on co-regulated chemokines [5]; BATF2 is strongly induced in interferon-γ-activated mouse macrophages [6].

## What the meta-analysis shows

The comparison pools four studies and identifies 947 significant genes at FDR < 0.05 among 16713 tested, with a median I² of 63.03%. No expected target genes were fixed in advance for this entity. The most significant estimates are TMC5 (log2 fold change +1.057, FDR 5.04e-113, three studies), HLA-DRA (−1.573, 7.43e-91, three studies), BATF2 (+3.189, 2.83e-76, two studies) and IFIH1 (+1.139, 4.11e-58, four studies); IFI6 follows (+1.366, 3.55e-33, four studies). Canonical IL-1 receptor targets are among the largest effects: CCL20 +4.186 (FDR 1.86e-02, four studies), IL6 +1.809 (2.02e-02, three studies), NFKBIA +1.592 (2.40e-02, four studies), IL36G +2.601 (3.93e-08, two studies), DEFB4A and DEFB4B +5.783 (1.56e-02, two studies) and complement factor B +3.571 (5.66e-04, two studies). Interferon-stimulated transcripts form a second block that splits by consistency: IFIH1 (I² 0.0%), IFI44L +1.888 (4.29e-14, I² 0.0%) and DHX58 +1.243 (2.02e-16, I² 0.0%) are estimated over three or four studies with negligible heterogeneity, whereas IFIT3 +3.054 (2.31e-02), OAS3 +1.896 (3.05e-02) and IFI44 +2.436 (4.93e-02) carry I² between 87.2% and 94.9%.

## Interpretation and limits

The estimates describe an IL-1-receptor-driven inflammatory programme superimposed on an interferon-stimulated one, and they should not be read as an IL-1α-specific signature: of the four pooled studies, two administer IL-1α and two IL-1β, one of the latter combined with IFN-γ, so the pooled set does not meet the requirement that all contributing studies measure the same contrast. Two further eligible studies contributed no data. The pooled weight is concentrated in a single experiment, GSE181229 — MDA-PCa-2b prostate carcinoma cells exposed to IL-1α at 25 ng/ml for three days — which carries 67.17%; the effective number of studies is 1.93, 48% of the nominal four. About two thirds of these estimates therefore describe one prostate carcinoma cell line. The biological material is not homogeneous: the other studies contribute non-lesional human skin, CD4+ T cells, and one comparison whose material is not stated. The interferon block cannot be assigned to a source here: the most reproducible interferon transcripts are estimated over three or four studies at I² near zero, so they cannot come from the single IFN-γ co-treated study alone, but an IL-1-driven component and study-specific contributions cannot be separated in this design. Several of the largest effects rest either on two studies only or on high between-study heterogeneity (CCL20 I² 91.5%, NFKBIA 95.7%, OAS3 94.9%).
